From the mathematics to a real decision

Learning objectives

  • Use a quadratic storage function to certify a coupled thermal model over a whole operating set.
  • Interpret an LMI through eigenvalues and distinguish its sufficient region from the physical constraint set.
  • Carry a disturbance bound into an invariant radius instead of claiming nominal convergence.

A commissioning decision

Two temperature sensors monitor adjacent zones of the robot enclosure. Define the dimensionless deviations $x_1=(T_1-40)/5$ and $x_2=(T_2-40)/5$, with temperatures in degrees Celsius. The permitted operating box is $|x_1|\le1$, $|x_2|\le1$, corresponding to 35–45 degrees in each zone. A candidate fixed controller has already been substituted into a linear minute-sampled model.

$$x_{t+1}=Ax_t,\qquad A=\begin{bmatrix}0.6&0.1\\0.1&0.7\end{bmatrix}.$$

Assume this map is exact inside the operating box, there is no disturbance for the initial calculation, and the equilibrium really is $(40,40)$ degrees. These assumptions make the example an analysis of a proposed closed loop, not a procedure for identifying its coefficients. The practical question is whether a specified family of initial temperatures stays inside the box while approaching equilibrium.

Use the quadratic energy $V(x)=x^\top Px$ with the proposed storage matrix $P=I$. Here $I$ is the two-dimensional identity. A positive definite storage weights directions in state space; it is not physical heat energy unless a separate physical derivation supplies that interpretation. We need its decrease, rather than that extra interpretation, for this decision.

Worked decision, with its limits

Form the residual. The Lyapunov inequality is $A^\top PA-P\prec0$. With $P=I$, multiplication gives

$$Q=I-A^\top A=\begin{bmatrix}0.63&-0.13\\-0.13&0.50\end{bmatrix},\qquad V(Ax)-V(x)=-x^\top Qx.$$

The two eigenvalues of $Q$ are approximately 0.419656 and 0.710344, both positive. Therefore the energy decreases for every nonzero vector, rather than only for a plotted trajectory. Equivalently, the largest eigenvalue of the symmetric positive definite $A$ is $q=0.761803$, so $\|Ax\|_2\le q\|x\|_2$. This establishes a contraction in the chosen Euclidean norm.

Connect energy to temperatures. If $V(x)\le1$, then each coordinate satisfies $|x_i|\le\|x\|_2\le1$. Thus the unit disk lies inside the physical operating box. Its invariance follows because $V$ decreases. The model was assumed only inside the box, but the induction remains valid: the initial state lies in the disk, the model maps it back into the disk, and the same assumption applies again.

Check one starting condition. At $x_0=(0.6,-0.8)$, the temperatures are $(43,36)$ degrees and $V(x_0)=1$. The next state is $(0.28,-0.50)$, giving temperatures $(41.4,37.5)$ and energy $0.28^2+0.50^2=0.3284$. The specific decrease exceeds the uniform guarantee because this vector is not aligned with the slowest contracting direction.

Read the limitation. The disk is a sufficient certified region. A corner such as $(1,-1)$ lies in the permitted box but outside this disk because its energy is 2. That does not mean it is unsafe; the certificate simply does not cover that starting point. To enlarge the region one could seek a different $P$ and prove its ellipsoid remains inside the box. Stability alone never supplies that containment argument.

A tempting wrong approach

Common mistake — Ignoring the coupling

Checking only $0.6\lt1$ and $0.7\lt1$ treats the sensors as independent scalar loops. Off-diagonal terms can defeat that reasoning: replacing both off-diagonal entries by 0.6 creates a symmetric matrix with a largest eigenvalue above 1. The original residual tests the complete map, including heat transferred between zones. Similarly, requiring every entry of $Q$ to be positive would reject a valid certificate because its off-diagonal entries are negative.

Transfer the argument

Exercise 2.B1 — Medium: Certify a rectangular startup specification

The startup procedure guarantees $|x_i|\le0.7$. Show that every allowed startup is covered by the disk certificate. Give a uniform coordinate bound at startup and after one update.

Review: Lyapunov matrix inequalities.

Show hint

Bound $V$ over the box, then apply the induced-norm contraction to its largest possible radius.

Show worked solution

Cover every permitted startup. Here $P=I$, so the disk certificate is $V(x)=x_1^2+x_2^2\le1$. For any startup satisfying $|x_1|,|x_2|\le0.7$, square the coordinate bounds and add:

$$V(x_0)\le(0.7)^2+(0.7)^2=0.49+0.49=0.98\le1.$$

The maximum $0.98$ is attained at each startup-box corner. Thus the whole startup box, including its boundary, lies in the certified invariant disk. The nominal model is assumed exact inside the operating box; disk containment and invariance keep every subsequent state in that domain.

Obtain a uniform bound from the storage. Each coordinate magnitude is at most the vector's Euclidean norm, since $x_i^2\le x_1^2+x_2^2$. Therefore the energy calculation gives

$$|x_{i,0}|\le\|x_0\|_2\le\sqrt{0.98}\approx0.989949.$$

The original startup specification supplies the sharper initial coordinate bound $0.7$. The radius bound is useful because it does not depend on the direction of the state.

Propagate the radius one step. The symmetric matrix $A$ has characteristic equation $\lambda^2-1.3\lambda+0.41=0$, with eigenvalues $(13\pm\sqrt5)/20$. Both are positive, so its Euclidean induced norm is the larger one, $q=(13+\sqrt5)/20\approx0.761803$. Consequently,

$$|x_{i,1}|\le\|Ax_0\|_2\le q\|x_0\|_2 \le q\sqrt{0.98}\approx0.754147.$$

This is a valid common coordinate bound after one update. If we also use the rectangular startup information directly, the triangle inequality gives $|x_{1,1}|\le0.6(0.7)+0.1(0.7)=0.49$ and $|x_{2,1}|\le0.1(0.7)+0.7(0.7)=0.56$. Thus $0.56$ is a sharper common one-step bound for this particular startup box; the storage bound remains convenient for subsequent arbitrary directions.

Read the temperature units. Since $T_i=40+5x_i$, a dimensionless coordinate bound $b$ means $40-5b\le T_i\le40+5b$ degrees Celsius. The startup bound $0.7$ gives $36.5$–$43.5$ degrees, and the direct common one-step bound $0.56$ gives $37.2$–$42.8$ degrees. All are inside the permitted $35$–$45$ range.

Exercise 2.B2 — Hard: Replace convergence by a disturbance tube

Now assume $x_{t+1}=Ax_t+w_t$ with $\|w_t\|_2\le0.05$ every minute. Derive an invariant Euclidean radius and decide whether the unit disk remains invariant. Can every trajectory still converge to zero?

Review: Storage and gain bounds.

Show hint

Use $\|Ax+w\|_2\le q\|x\|_2+0.05$. Consider a constant disturbance along a unit eigenvector for $q$.

Show worked solution

Include the disturbance at every step. Let $r_t=\|x_t\|_2$. The induced-norm bound for the stated $A$, followed by the triangle inequality, gives

$$r_{t+1}=\|Ax_t+w_t\|_2 \le\|Ax_t\|_2+\|w_t\|_2 \le q r_t+0.05,\qquad q=\frac{13+\sqrt5}{20}\lt1.$$

To make a disk of radius $R\ge0$ invariant, we need the right-hand side to be at most $R$ whenever $r_t\le R$. Since $q\ge0$, its largest value on that disk is $qR+0.05$. Solve

$$qR+0.05\le R \iff 0.05\le(1-q)R \iff R\ge R_*:=\frac{0.05}{1-q} =\frac{1}{7-\sqrt5} =\frac{7+\sqrt5}{44}\approx0.209911.$$

Division preserves the inequality because $1-q\gt0$. Every radius $R\ge R_*$ passes this norm test. In particular, for $R=1$, $qR+0.05\approx0.811803\le1$, so the unit disk remains invariant for every disturbance in the given ball. As that disk lies in the operating box, induction also keeps the local model assumption applicable.

Show the transient and ultimate bounds. Iterating the scalar inequality yields $r_1\le qr_0+0.05$, $r_2\le q^2r_0+0.05(q+1)$, and in general

$$r_t\le q^t r_0+0.05\sum_{j=0}^{t-1}q^j =q^t r_0+\frac{0.05(1-q^t)}{1-q} =R_*+q^t(r_0-R_*).$$

The sum follows by multiplying the finite geometric sum by $q$ and subtracting it from the original sum. Because $q^t\to0$, this proves $\limsup_{t\to\infty}r_t\le R_*$. If a state starts outside the smallest disk, the bound approaches its radius; it does not assert entry into that exact disk in a finite number of steps.

Why convergence to zero is no longer guaranteed. Take a unit eigenvector $v$ of $A$ with $Av=qv$, and the allowed constant disturbance $w_t=0.05v$. At $x_*=R_*v$ we have

$$Ax_*+w_t=(qR_*+0.05)v=R_*v=x_*.$$

This nonzero equilibrium lies inside the unit disk and satisfies the model assumptions. Starting there produces a trajectory that never converges to zero. It also shows that no smaller centered Euclidean disk is robustly invariant: at the point $Rv$ with $R\lt R_*$ the same disturbance makes the next radius $qR+0.05\gt R$. The justified conclusion is an invariant disturbance tube and an ultimate bound. Convergence to zero would need further conditions, for example disturbances tending to zero.

Synthesis and bridge

An LMI is useful when its matrix inequality closes the whole decision chain: decrease of storage, an invariant sublevel set, containment in physical limits, and applicability of the model on that set. Numerical feasibility is evidence for only one link. The calculation also shows how the same storage can answer a revised commissioning request after disturbances are introduced.
